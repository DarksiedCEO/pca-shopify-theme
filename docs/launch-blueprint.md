## 2. Above-the-Fold Copy

**Announcement bar** (rotates every 4–5s, two messages max):
1. `NEW: The Rare Ones Wardrobe is live — Buy 2, Save 15% · Buy 3, Save 20%`
2. `Free shipping on orders over $65`

**Hero headline — recommendation and rationale**

You gave five directions. Scored against the Constitution's "so what?" test and 3-second clarity standard:

| Candidate | Read |
|---|---|
| "The Rare Ones Wardrobe" | Reads as a collection title, not an emotional headline. Good secondary use (collection page H1), weak as the hero's emotional lead. |
| "Luxury Layers for Hairless Cats" | Maximum clarity, minimum emotion — informational, not aspirational. Better as subheadline. |
| "For Cats Who Were Never Ordinary" | Strong emotion, but fails the 3-second clarity test alone — a cold visitor doesn't yet know this is clothing. |
| "Softness. Warmth. Presence." | Beautiful editorial rhythm (this is your Chanel-campaign register), but zero product clarity without a strong subhead underneath it. |
| "Wardrobe for the Rare Ones" | Clear category (wardrobe) + on-brand emotional anchor ("Rare Ones") in one line. Closest to doing both jobs at once. |

**Final recommendation — don't pick one, stack two of them correctly:**

> **Headline:** Wardrobe for the Rare Ones.
> **Subheadline:** Luxury layers cut for Sphynx, Devon Rex, and every hairless breed — for cats who were never ordinary.

This gives you clarity (headline: wardrobe, category, brand) and emotion (subhead: "never ordinary," breed specificity that also does double duty as SEO/relevance for hairless-cat owners searching for exactly this).

**Primary CTA:** `Shop The Rare Ones Wardrobe`
**Secondary CTA:** `Find Your Cat's Size` — routes trust-conscious visitors to the size guide instead of losing them; a second CTA that just repeats "Shop Now" wastes the slot.

**Trust microcopy under CTA:** `Curated for hairless breeds · Secure checkout · Ships in [X–X] days`

**Discount teaser (small, refined — not a banner):** `Dressing more than one? Buy 2, save 15%.`

## 4. Offer Architecture

| Offer | Mechanism | Wording |
|---|---|---|
| Buy 2, Save 15% | **Automatic** discount (Shopify native quantity-based automatic discount, storefront-applied at cart) | `You're saving 15%.` |
| Buy 3, Save 20% | **Automatic**, same mechanism, higher tier | `Add 1 more to unlock 20% off — you're saving 15% right now.` |
| Bath Bonnet add-on | **Automatic** line-item price adjustment when a wardrobe item is already in cart (cart-drawer tile, not a code) | `Complete the ritual — add the Bath Bonnet for $11.99.` |
| Starter Wardrobe bundle | Curated 2-item fixed bundle, small badge, not a clearance frame | `The Rare Ones Starter Set — Turtleneck + Bath Bonnet, $38.98 (save $6.00)` |
| Free shipping | **Automatic**, threshold-based shipping rate | `Free shipping over $65` |

**Automatic vs. code — recommendation: automatic, no exceptions at launch.** For a brand-new store, every extra step (find a code, copy it, paste it, hope it works) is a trust tax you can't afford yet. Automatic discounts also read as "this is just the honest price," which is itself a trust signal — nothing to hunt for, nothing that feels like a trick.

**Why $65 for free shipping, specifically:** a two-item cart (e.g., Turtleneck $29.99 × 2 = $59.98) sits just under it; adding the Bath Bonnet add-on ($11.99) clears it at $71.97. The threshold is calibrated to reward exactly the multi-item behavior you're trying to build, not set arbitrarily. **This number needs to be checked against real carrier cost before launch** — see Red-Team §12.

**Starter Wardrobe rationale:** a first-time buyer of an unknown 6-SKU brand faces real choice paralysis. A single pre-curated "start here" bundle gives risk-averse cold traffic a decisive, low-commitment entry point without looking like a clearance bin — the discount is small and framed as curation, not a bargain.

## 5. Merchandising Order (and why)

1. **Rare Skin Turtleneck™** — leads. Lowest price point, broadest breed appeal, easiest "yes" for cold traffic. Establishes the Rare Skin sub-line first.
2. **Alley Scholar Collection™** — second, even at the highest price point. This is deliberate price-anchoring: putting the hero luxury piece early lifts the perceived value of everything that follows it. It's also your strongest "not a dropship store" visual proof.
3. **Rare Skin Lounge Hoodie™** — natural next-add within the Rare Skin line; keeps momentum from #1.
4. **Rare One Winter Vest™** — seasonal layering cross-sell, positioned after the core pieces are established.
5. **Alley Stripe Summer Suit™** — shows range and depth (signals "wardrobe brand," not "one product store").
6. **Rare Skin Bath Bonnet™** — last. Its job is attach-rate on an order already in motion, not to be a headline demand-driver.

## 6. Luxury Visual Art Direction

**Color**
- Obsidian Black `#0B0B0C` — primary background
- Soft Ivory `#F5F0E6` — secondary background / breathing room sections
- Antique Gold `#B08D57` — CTAs, dividers, icon strokes, hover states — used sparingly, never as a fill color for large blocks
- Deep Charcoal `#1C1B1A` — body text on ivory, secondary surfaces
- Shadows: warm-toned, low-opacity charcoal — never a pure black drop shadow, which reads flat and cheap

**Typography**
- Display/headline: serif (Cormorant Garamond, per existing theme setup) — italic used sparingly for emotional emphasis lines
- Body/UI/price/labels: clean geometric sans (DM Sans)
- All-caps labels ("SHOP THE COLLECTION") get generous letter-spacing — tracked-out caps read luxury, tight caps read discount-rack

**Photography / video mood**
- Shallow depth of field, editorial studio lighting (soft top-key + rim light)
- Slow, deliberate motion — no fast cuts
- Muted, warm color grade
- Generous negative space around the subject — don't crowd the frame
- No bright, saturated "pet store" backgrounds

**Spacing & layout**
- Minimum 96–120px vertical section padding on desktop
- Product grid: 2-up or 3-up max on desktop — a dense 4+ column grid reads cheap regardless of copy quality
- Alternate full-bleed imagery with symmetric copy sections to avoid visual monotony
- Prefer asymmetric editorial layouts (image-left/text-right, alternating) over rigid centered grids

**Product cards**
- Image, name, price, one descriptor line — nothing else
- No badge clutter, no fake star-rating widget (there are no reviews yet — don't imply there are)
- Hover reveals a second image (motion), not a "quick add" popup

**Buttons & icons**
- Rectangular or minimally rounded (2–4px radius) — pill-shaped buttons read DTC/dropship, avoid them
- Antique gold fill on dark backgrounds; obsidian fill on ivory backgrounds
- Icons: thin single-weight line icons only, gold or charcoal stroke, no filled emoji-style icons

**Mobile**
- Single column, hero video muted-autoplay with a tap-to-unmute control
- Sticky mobile add-to-cart bar (product name + price + button) appears once the user scrolls past the primary CTA on PDPs — this is a Constitution mobile-first requirement, not optional polish

**What to avoid entirely**
- Countdown timers, "X people viewing this" popups, fake stock-scarcity indicators (Constitution hard limit — these are rejected regardless of requested conversion lift)
- Bright red "SALE" stickers
- Cluttered badge rows
- Generic stock photography of cats
- Auto-playing sound

## 7. Trust-Building System (New Store, Zero Reviews)

The Constitution forbids fake reviews and fake social proof outright, so the trust stack has to substitute *real* signals instead of manufactured ones:

- **Founder's Note** — first-person, specific, photographed (real founder, not stock), placed mid-page. Why the brand exists, the founder's connection to hairless-cat care, and the quality obsession behind how pieces are chosen. Do not claim personal ownership of hairless cats unless confirmed true — use founder-led-brand language until/unless that fact is verified.
- **Brand Promise block** — 3–4 concrete, true claims only. Examples in this brand's register: "Every piece selected and fit-checked specifically for hairless and thin-coated breeds, not repurposed generic pet apparel," "Sized by weight and measurement, not guesswork," "Every piece hand-inspected before it ships." Do not claim fabric testing unless testing actually occurred. Do not include anything resembling a veterinary or medical claim — the existing theme's trust rules (no invented medical claims) apply storewide, not just to the fountain page.
- **Sizing confidence** — visible size chart plus a direct "measure once, email us if unsure" support-first framing.
- **Secure checkout** — real Shopify/Shop Pay/card-network icons only. No invented "trust seal" graphics.
- **"Ask before you order"** — a visible email/chat prompt specifically framed for first-time buyers of an unfamiliar brand.
- **Transparent shipping** — exact processing and transit windows, not "fast shipping."
- **"New Brand, Real Craft" section — the single highest-leverage move available here.** Instead of hiding the absence of reviews, own it directly: *"This is our first drop. There are no reviews yet — because no one's worn it yet. Here's exactly what went into it."* This preempts the "why no reviews" objection instead of hoping the visitor doesn't notice it. See Red-Team §12 for why this section is the one most likely to get cut internally, and why it shouldn't be.

## 10. Video Hero — Creative Brief

**Title:** "For The Rare Ones" — Hero Film
**Length:** 8–12s looping cut for the homepage hero (mobile-safe file size); 15–20s extended cutdown for an About/brand page.

**Opening 3 seconds:** Close macro shot of fabric texture (camel wool weave, or fleece) grazed by soft studio light. Rack focus pulls to reveal a Sphynx cat's ear or shoulder wearing the piece. No logo yet — texture first, presence second.

**Body (middle):** 3–4 slow-motion shots:
- Cat turning its head in the obsidian dress shirt against an ivory backdrop
- Cat walking slowly across a dark set, rim-lit silhouette
- Macro detail on the antique-gold tie clip or vest button
- A Bath Bonnet moment, shot calm and natural (not comedic) — cat relaxed, ears covered, a water droplet on charcoal tile

**Pacing:** Unhurried, editorial cadence. No fast cuts. Each shot holds 1.5–2.5s minimum, shot at 96–120fps and graded to roughly 40–50% speed.

**Music mood:** Minimal, moody, string- or piano-led — closer to a fashion-house campaign score than upbeat "cute pet content" audio.

**Text overlays:** Twice, maximum. Mid-film: `For The Rare Ones.` (serif, gold on obsidian, fade in/out). End card: `Shop The Rare Ones Wardrobe →`.

**CTA placement:** Only in the final 2–3 seconds as a static end-card, or as a persistent on-page button below the video — not burned into the entire clip, which reads as an ad rather than a brand film.

**Closing 3 seconds:** Cat settles, camera holds still, wordmark fades in center, CTA card. No audio sting — let it breathe.

**Product moments to include across the cut:** Turtleneck close-up, Scholar Jacket full shot, Bath Bonnet in use, one shot showing 2–3 colorways in quick succession or in-frame together.

## 12. Red-Team Audit — Where This Plan Can Still Fail

- **No reviews is a real risk, not just a framing problem.** "Own the newness" copy helps, but cold paid traffic to a zero-review $25–55 apparel brand is a genuinely hard sell. Mitigate by prioritizing warm/retargeting audiences before cold prospecting spend, and get real customer UGC into rotation the moment first orders ship — don't wait for a "critical mass" of reviews before using any of it.
- **Scholar Collection and Winter Vest pricing is still a placeholder.** If real pricing lands meaningfully higher than the Turtleneck/Hoodie tier, the "luxury but still reasonable" balance in this whole plan could break. Lock pricing before finalizing any copy.
- **Weight-based sizing on the Winter Vest is a return-risk generator.** Weight doesn't capture body shape — two cats can share a weight and not share a fit. Pair the weight chart with a chest-girth measurement, and make the FAQ explicit that borderline cases should email before ordering.
- **The no-return policy on worn apparel is a legitimate cart-abandonment risk** for a first-time buyer of an unfamiliar brand, "email us first" framing notwithstanding. Consider a one-time size-exchange path (not a return, a exchange credit or replacement) as risk reversal that doesn't violate the hygiene constraint. This is a business-owner pricing/policy decision, not something to add to copy unilaterally — flagging it, not deciding it.
- **Automatic Buy 2/Buy 3 discounts could compress margin faster than expected** on items already priced at $24.99–32.99. Confirm real unit economics before locking the 15%/20% tiers storewide — if margin is thin, consider gating the 20% tier to higher-ticket categories (Scholar Collection, Winter Vest) instead of every SKU.
- **The $65 free-shipping threshold needs real carrier-cost math**, not just cart-composition math. If actual parcel shipping cost for a garment order exceeds what a $65 order nets after discount and payment processing, this bleeds margin on every qualifying order.
- **The mandatory video-first hero is also the single biggest mobile performance risk.** An unoptimized autoplay hero video is the most common killer of mobile Core Web Vitals, and this launch depends entirely on cold-traffic conversion — the exact audience most sensitive to load speed. Non-negotiable before launch: compressed file (<3MB), poster-frame fallback, lazy-loaded below-hero content, and a real-device 4G test. Don't ship "beautiful" un-benchmarked.
- **The Bath Bonnet (ear-covering apparel on an animal) carries real supervision/injury liability optics.** The FAQ language here is a good start but deserves an actual policy/legal review pass, not just conversion-copy judgment.
- **"Sold separately" on the Scholar Collection could quietly undercut the Buy 2/Buy 3 strategy** if customers perceive the tie or a shirt as a minor "accessory" rather than a real item worth stacking toward a discount tier. Every Scholar piece needs to read as a standalone hero in its own copy, not an add-on, to stay eligible in the buyer's mental math.
- **The "New Brand, Real Craft" trust section is the one most likely to get cut in internal review** because admitting there are no reviews yet can feel like a weakness to spotlight. It isn't — for this specific store, at this specific trust level, it's a bigger lever than another badge row. Keep it.
- **Every remaining `[bracket]` in this document is a launch blocker, not a nice-to-have.** This blueprint is not ready to hand to a developer until processing time, support email, return window, and final variable pricing are filled in with real values.
